## 2. Cleaning rules

### 2.1 Missing values: required, expected, optional

A value is **missing** when the field/element is absent, empty, or one of the placeholders `-`, `--`, `0000-00-00`, `0000-00-00 00:00`, `?`, `n/a`, `na`, `nil value`, `null` (and `-1` in numeric vital signs). The words `aucun`, `aucune`, `nil`, `nil known`, `nka`, `nkda`, `no known allergies`, `no known drug allergies`, `no known drug allergy`, `none`, `rien` in allergies/medications mean **none** and are kept as the value `none`, not as missing.

- **Required** - missing or invalid -> the record is **rejected** (quarantined). These are the ID, the references to the parent records and the clinical facts that define the record. We never invent them.
- **Expected** - missing -> record **retained**, value NULL, row flagged `missing_expected:<field>`. Used when rejecting would destroy other valid facts (e.g. a staff member with no name: many clinical records point to that staff ID).
- **Optional** - missing -> NULL. Invalid optional values (e.g. a pulse of 420) are set to NULL and flagged; the record is kept.
- **Corrected** - only when the right value is certain from the data itself: format, unit, case, whitespace, ICD-9 punctuation, references to a merged duplicate, a diagnosis patient taken from its admission.

### 2.2 Duplicates

- **Exact duplicate**: same ID and every field equal after trimming spaces and ignoring case -> the first copy is kept, the others are removed (listed in the quarantine as `duplicate_removed`). The same ID with *different* content is a conflict -> the later copy is rejected (`duplicate_id_conflict`).
- **Duplicate entity (near duplicate)**: different IDs, same entity key after cleaning (dates parsed, labels mapped, case ignored) **and no other field with two different non-empty values** -> the lowest ID is kept, the other is removed, missing values of the kept record are filled from it, and every reference to the removed ID is re-pointed to the kept one.
- Same key but a conflicting value elsewhere (e.g. two exams of the same type at the same minute for different orders, two notes at the same minute by different authors) -> both are real records: both kept and flagged `possible_duplicate`.
- Staff have no date of birth, so name + role + department is a weak key: two such staff records are merged unless BOTH IDs are used by 10+ clinical records; then they are most likely two people with the same name -> both kept and flagged `possible_duplicate`. Keys:

### 2.3 Text

- Whitespace: trimmed and runs of spaces collapsed to one space, in every text field.
- Person names: each word capitalised (`DUBOIS` -> `Dubois`, `o'neill` -> `O'Neill`), particles in lower case (`de`, `van`, `der`, `bin`, `binte`, `s/o`, `d/o`). Source A writes given name + family name; Source C writes Chinese names family-first and Malay/Indian names given-first; B and D give one name field (D sometimes as `SURNAME, Given`, reordered to `Given Surname`). `full_name` follows each hospital's convention.
- Short descriptive text (reasons, departments, indications, allergies...): the spelling used most often for the same value in that column is applied to all its variants (`CHEST PAIN`, `cHEST pAIN` -> `Chest pain`). Symptoms are lower case. Room/bed codes upper case.
- Clinical free text (notes, findings, instructions, addresses) keeps its case; only whitespace is normalised, so nothing a clinician wrote is changed.
- Phones -> E.164 (`+33612345678`, `+6591234567`); the emergency contact is split into name and phone.

### 2.4 Dates and timestamps

| source | hospital | format | time zone | raw timestamps are | extract time (local) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | CHU de Montclair-sur-Loire | TXT | Europe/Paris | local wall-clock time, no offset (DD/MM/YYYY HH:MM) | 2025-10-31 18:00 |
| B | Uganda National Hospital | CSV | Africa/Kampala | local wall-clock time, no offset (DD/MM/YYYY HH:MM; slash dates with AM/PM are month-first) | 2025-10-31 18:00 |
| C | Straits Regional Medical Centre | XML | Asia/Singapore | ISO 8601 with +08:00 (some with Z = UTC, some without offset = local) | 2025-10-31 18:00 |
| D | Coral Coast Health Service | JSON | Australia/Sydney | ISO 8601 with +10:00/+11:00 (daylight saving); also Z, Unix seconds/milliseconds, local day-first slash dates, dot dates in either order | 2025-10-31 18:00 |

- Every timestamp is stored in **UTC**, ISO 8601 (`2024-05-08T12:59:00Z`); dates of birth as `YYYY-MM-DD`. `admissions.source_timezone` keeps the hospital's zone so local time can be recovered.
- Formats read: `DD/MM/YYYY HH:MM`, `DD/MM/YYYY 14h59`, `DD.MM.YYYY HH:MM`, ISO with or without `T`, offset or `Z`, `08 May 2024 02:59 PM`, `5 mars 1980` / `05-Mar-1980` (French and English month names), Unix seconds / milliseconds (D). Source conventions: slash dates are day-first, except B slash dates with AM/PM, which are month-first; dot dates are day-first in A but come in both orders in D.
- Dash dates `NN-NN-YYYY`: day-first if the first number is over 12, month-first if the second is over 12; if both are 12 or less (and differ) the date is **ambiguous** and is not guessed -> rejected when the field is required, NULL + flag otherwise. The same rule applies to D's dot dates. Impossible dates (`31/02`, month 13, hour 25, a year outside 1900-2100 such as `0202` or `20225`, two-digit years, `a preciser`, `TBC`) -> invalid, same treatment.
- A timestamp with no offset is read in the source's zone. Europe/Paris has daylight saving time: a time in the autumn repeated hour (02:00-02:59 on the last Sunday of October) is **ambiguous**; we take the first occurrence (summer time, UTC+2) and flag it `dst_ambiguous_time_assumed_first`. A time in the spring gap does not exist; it is shifted forward one hour and flagged. Australia/Sydney has DST too (AEST UTC+10 / AEDT UTC+11, October to April) and gets the same treatment. Kampala (UTC+3) and Singapore (UTC+8) have no DST. A date of birth sent as a Unix instant (D) is converted to the local date in Sydney. A value with an explicit offset or `Z` is converted exactly. An unknown time zone would be a configuration error (the run stops) rather than a guess.

### 2.5 Numbers and units (triage vital signs)

- Decimal comma -> point; unit text removed (`88 bpm`, `96%`, `4/10`, `120/80 mmHg`, `120-80`).
- Temperature -> Celsius. The unit comes from the XML `unit` attribute, else from the text (`F`, `°F`, `C`), else from the value: 86-113 can only be Fahrenheit for a living patient, 30-45 only Celsius.
- SpO2 -> percent: Source C stores a fraction (`0.97` -> 97); a value written with `%` is already a percent.
- Blood pressure split into `systolic_bp` / `diastolic_bp`.
- After conversion, values outside these ranges are **invalid** -> NULL + flag `out_of_range:<field>`; non-numbers -> NULL + flag `invalid_number:<field>` (vitals are optional, the triage record is kept):

| field | min | max |
|---|---|---|
| systolic_bp | 50 | 260 |
| diastolic_bp | 20 | 160 |
| heart_rate | 20 | 250 |
| respiratory_rate | 4 | 60 |
| temperature_c | 30.0 | 45.0 |
| spo2_pct | 50 | 100 |
| pain_score | 0 | 10 |

- ICD-9: trimmed, upper case, leading zero and decimal point restored; a code not in the Phase 1 ICD-9 dictionary is **rejected** (`unknown_icd9_code`) - we do not replace it with another code.

### 2.7 Relationships

- References are checked parent-first: professionals, patients, admissions, orders, radiology, then the other admission children.
- `orphan_reference`: the referenced ID does not exist anywhere in the source -> rejected.
- `parent_quarantined`: the parent exists but was rejected -> the child is rejected too (it cannot be loaded without its parent). Counted as *cascade* in section 1.
- `inconsistent_reference`: a diagnosis whose patient differs from its admission's patient; a radiology exam whose admission differs from its order's admission -> rejected. A radiology exam with no order is a legitimate walk-in exam (`order_id` NULL); one pointing to a non-existent order is rejected, because blanking the reference would turn it into a walk-in exam, which is a fact we do not know.
- In Source C the parent admission of a nested record is its enclosing `<admission>`; records in `<unlinkedRecords>` use their `admissionRef`. A staff member with the wrong role for a field (e.g. a triage nurse who is not a nurse) is flagged `role_mismatch`.

### 2.8 Chronology (all comparisons in UTC)

Rejected:
- date of birth after the extract date; admission before the patient's date of birth; admission or discharge after the extract time; discharge before admission;
- triage, diagnosis, note, order, radiology exam, procedure, unit-stay entry and discharge record before the admission or after its discharge (after the extract time for an active admission);
- unit exit before unit entry or after the admission's discharge; radiology exam before its order; a discharge record for an admission that is still active.

Flagged only (kept): discharge-record time different from the admission's discharge time, follow-up before discharge, overlapping unit stays, open unit stay after discharge, status `active` with a discharge time (or discharged without one), patient `alive` with an admission that ended in death, age over 120.
